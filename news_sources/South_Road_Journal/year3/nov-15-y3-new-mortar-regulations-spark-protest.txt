The South Road Journal- November 15, Year 3

NEW MORTAR REGULATIONS SPARK PROTEST

Heliastes, SRJ Editor-in-Chief

   New regulations targeting compliance with mortar mixing specifications had local builders wielding their torches and trowels and marching down to the capital this week to protest the changes. "This is ridiculous, these bureaucrats literally doubled our cost of materials overnight without even holding a single meeting for public comment!" said theinsaneeraser, a building contractor based out of Frost Peak. "I have to feed my family with the meager margin I make on brickwork; how's the math going to work out now?!"
   Government regulators disagreed, stating that there was an urgent need to address outstanding safety issues. "When we go out and inspect brickwork for code compliance, we find that, year after year, bricklayers increasingly skimp on the amount of mortar required to form structurally-sound buildings," said SteelCryptid, code compliance officer. "It might stand for a few months on its own, but the moment even a light temporal storm arises, the thin mortar is unable to withstand even weak temporal-seismic waves and the structure literally crumbles apart."
   Even homeowners are feeling the pinch. "When I got a quote back in May of this year, the price for a new one story brick home was 200 RG," said AmberAfternoon, prospective homebuyer. "When the contractor came back with the finalized quote last week, the price had jumped to 500 RG! He said that not only did the mortar needed for the project cost more, but that he would have to go out and manually produce the mortar himself as there was now a massive shortage of lime. I can't even go with a traditional plaster home anymore since that uses lime too!" She added, "I guess I have to just go back to renting a dirt townhouse in TwoNutsHeim again."
